# Hunters Point Edition — series brief (read after station-brief.md)

The Hunters Point Edition is a 25-station flagship built to be offered to the
Marie Harrison Community Foundation ("Can We Live?"), a 501(c)(3) in Bayview
Hunters Point founded by Executive Director Arieann Harrison in honour of her
mother Marie Harrison (1948–2019), the neighbourhood's environmental-justice
leader. Its programmes: the Community Pollution Patrol Network, the Hunters
Point Biomonitoring Initiative, an academic scholarship, Solutions for Women,
YES Camp, BaySpark and the Bayview Hunters Point Air Monitor Project (ten
monitors). Read `WebXR/smartcity/js/sims/can-we-live-story.js` — the sourced
opener — before writing a word; it is the only place facts about the
foundation, its founder or the site appear.

## Rules that are not negotiable
- Every station is sited generically ("a parcel under a federal cleanup
  order", "a street beside a fenced shipyard"). Never place a scene on the
  real site, never name a real person, never invent a foundation programme
  detail, a partner, a number or a clause. The foundation is context in the
  `certification` line and the programme `why`; it is not a character.
- The edition trains what community science and the trades beside it do; it
  is not the foundation's own programme and must never say it is.
- Consent and chain of custody are procedure, not decoration: any station
  that touches a person's sample or data carries informed consent (45 CFR
  46) as a scored step.
- Patrols work from the public side of the fence. Going inside is a hazard
  unless the station is explicitly a trained-worker station (HAZWOPER).

Authorities that genuinely apply: BAAQMD complaint and Community Advisory
Council process; EPA QA/QC (QAPP, chain of custody) and Superfund community
involvement; California DTSC and the Regional Water Board; OSHA 29 CFR
1910.120 HAZWOPER and 1910.134 respirators; 45 CFR 46 informed consent; Cal/OSHA
wildfire-smoke rule; NIOSH; LIUNA and IUOE for the site-work pathway.
