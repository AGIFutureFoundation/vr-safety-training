# First Responder series — series brief (read after station-brief.md)

Fifteen stations for the people who run toward the call: firefighters
(IAFF), EMS crews (IAFF, NAGE and AFSCME EMS locals), police officers
(police officer associations and the FOP), social workers and crisis
counsellors (NASW, SEIU 1021), and disaster-relief teams (AFSCME public
works, LIUNA, the Red Cross's volunteer workforce). Category "Emergency
Services", district as fits or `indoor` for a station, dispatch centre or
shelter. Siblings: triage-point.js, ev-extrication.js, chairside-emergency.js,
patron-deescalation.js, wvpp-panic-button.js.

## Emotional intelligence is procedure here, not decoration
Every station in this series carries the human side as scored steps: the
words said to a person in crisis, the check on a partner, the debrief, the
peer-support referral. Rules:
- The person in crisis is a person: named by role ("the patient", "the
  caller", "the resident"), never mocked, never a prop. No gore, no graphic
  injury detail; the scene shows equipment, positions and decisions.
- Language the trade actually uses for de-escalation and trauma-informed
  care (calm voice, distance, one speaker, choices offered, time given,
  needs named) appears in the step `cue` and `why`, so the learner practises
  the words, not only the actions.
- Each station ends with a two-part close: the operational log, and a
  short check-in (how the crew is, the peer-support or EAP line named, the
  critical-incident stress rule the department uses). The engine's own
  guide (shared/ei-guide.js) adds a supportive line after a hazard or a
  missed interruption; write `wrongNote`/`missNote` text in that voice —
  what happened and what to do next, never blame.
- Interruptions are the human ones: a bystander filming, a partner
  freezing, a family member arriving, a second caller, a crew member
  showing signs of heat or stress.

Authorities that genuinely apply: NFPA 1500, 1584 (rehab), 1710, 1001; OSHA
29 CFR 1910.134 and 1910.156 (fire brigades), 1910.1030; NHTSA National
EMS Scope of Practice; CIT International's crisis intervention team model;
NASW Code of Ethics; SAMHSA trauma-informed care principles; Psychological
First Aid (NCTSN/WHO); NIMS/ICS (FEMA IS-100/700); the Red Cross shelter
standards; Cal/OSHA 8 CCR 3342 for workplace violence in clinics.
