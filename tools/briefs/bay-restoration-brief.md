# Bay Restoration & Cleanup brief — 35 maritime and underwater stations

The user asked for thirty-five more simulation training areas for deep San Francisco Bay restoration and bay clean-up, weighted to maritime and underwater jobs and their safety. They form one new programme, **SF Bay Restoration & Cleanup — Maritime and Underwater** (`bay-restoration-maritime-underwater`), in five packs of seven. `station-brief.md`, `ladder-brief.md` §Content rules, `assets-brief.md` and `bayarea-brief.md` (dive and marine language rules) all still bind.

## Ground already covered — do not rebuild it
`mw-*` (dive supervisor and plan, diver emergency and recovery, hull inspection dive, pier pile inspection dive, underwater welding and cutting, workboat towing, ferry deckhand, oil transfer watch and boom), `eelgrass-transplant`, `oyster-reef-monitoring`, `living-shoreline`, `tide-gate`, `marsh-transect-survey`, `spill-boom-deploy`, `dredge-barge`, `sediment-cap`, `shoreline-sediment-grab`, `stormwater-outfall`, `creosote-pile-removal`, `ballast-water-sampling`, `spartina-removal`, `bioswale-build`, `hunters-point`, the `rad-*` and `building-rad-scan` stations, `mooring-line`, `pilot-transfer`, `bunkering-watch`. A new station may share a setting with one of these but must teach a different job.

## Rules particular to this wave
- **Dive**: depth, gas, bottom time and decompression are never stated as numbers; they are "per the dive plan" / "per the tables the supervisor holds". Every dive station has a standby diver, a tender, a supervisor and a comms check as steps or crew. Cite OSHA 29 CFR 1910 Subpart T and the ADCI consensus standard as bodies; the dive contractor's own safe-practices manual is a body called "the employer's safe practices manual".
- **Vessel**: USCG rules by part (33 CFR, 46 CFR) as registry bodies; a PFD is worn on deck in every step; MOB recovery follows the vessel's drill, not an invented timing.
- **Hunters Point and radiological**: only facts already in this repo (`hunters-point.js`, `rad-*`); no dose numbers, no named people, no claims about the cleanup's status. Screening decisions are "per the work plan".
- **Ecology**: species named only at the level of "harbor seal", "harbor porpoise", "Ridgway's rail", "Pacific herring", with the seasonal work window "per the permit". Marine Mammal Protection Act, the Endangered Species Act, BCDC and the Regional Water Quality Control Board are bodies; FAA Part 107 for the drone station.
- **Unions**: ILWU and the Inlandboatmen's Union for deck and terminal work, MEBA and SIU for engine and unlicensed crew, Pile Drivers Local 34 (UBC) for divers and pile work, IUOE Local 3 for dredge and excavator, LIUNA Local 261 for hazmat and shoreline crews, AFSCME and SEIU for agency scientists. Name the union as the training body; never assert a union's endorsement of this software.
- **Scenes**: dive stations use `district: "bay-underwater"` where the work is underwater and a workboat deck for the surface stations; marine stations use the Bay and its shoreline, never the Golden Gate deck (that is the bridge programme's).
- **Assets**: vessels, cranes, excavators and trucks come from `fleet.js`/`equipment.js`; a workboat, skiff, barge or skimmer that no builder makes yet is added to `fleet.js` as a builder (authored count documented in `docs/fleet.md`), never inlined.
- **Hand-off**: teams do **not** edit `curricula.js`. Each pack writes `tools/briefs/bay-restoration.pack-<letter>.json`: `[{ "id", "why" }]` with one-sentence `why` per station; the integrator assembles the programme entry.
