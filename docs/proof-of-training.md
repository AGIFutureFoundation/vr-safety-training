# Proof of training: the mastery rule, competencies and what gets exported

A station badge says a learner played a station well. That is motivation, and it is not what a union hall, a JATC or a safety director signs. What they sign is a **competency**: a named thing a journeyman can do, tied to standards a body actually publishes, demonstrated by named stations under one rule that never bends.

This layer is `WebXR/shared/competency.js` (pure data and pure functions over the training record — no DOM, no storage, so `tools/check_competency.mjs` runs all of it in Node), the **Proof** tab of the Training Records overlay in SmartCiti.X, and the exports below. It sits above `WebXR/shared/records.js` (the attempt log) and beside `WebXR/smartcity/js/gamify.js` (the per-station badges, which are unchanged — competency badges are a second, sober tier and never replace them).

## The mastery rule

> A run demonstrates mastery when it earns **two or more stars**, records **zero unsafe actions**, **answers every interruption** it was given, and finishes **within 1.5 × the station's par time**. One mastery run on enough of a competency's stations earns "demonstrated"; mastery runs on three different days earn "consistent". No other rule earns a competency.

It is deliberately stricter than the pass rule in `records.js`. A pass is "you got through it" (2 stars, no unsafe action). Mastery is "you got through it the way the standard describes", and adds the interruptions and the time ceiling.

| Condition | Field on the record | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 2 stars | `stars` | The engine's 2-star band is already "at most one correction and inside 1.5× par" (`game.js` `finish()`). |
| 0 unsafe actions | `hazardHits` | An unsafe action is disqualifying, full stop. A wrong or unanswered interruption counts as one — `game.js` `resolveInterrupt()` increments `hazardHits` for both. |
| Every interruption answered | `interrupts: { answered, wrong, missed }` | Running the procedure and noticing the alarm are two different competencies; this tier demands both. `null` means no interruption fired, which is nothing to answer rather than a failure. |
| Time ≤ 1.5 × par | `seconds`, `parSeconds` | The same multiple the 2-star band uses. Where no par is known the time limit cannot be applied and the other three conditions decide. |

`isMastery(record, parSeconds)` is the rule. `masteryShortfall(record, parSeconds)` is the same evaluation returning *why* a run fell short — one reason, the first that applies, in the order above, because a learner who touched a live bus and also ran long needs to hear about the live bus. That reason is what the transcript prints against a near miss, and it is the reason this layer exists at all: "fail" teaches nothing, "one interruption missed of two" teaches the next run.

**Demonstrated** means `require` of a competency's stations each carry at least one mastery run. **Consistent** means demonstrated *and* mastery runs on three or more different days — three runs in one sitting is one session, not consistency. A competency is dated by its evidence: `earnedAt` is the timestamp of the `require`-th station's first mastery run, so an exported badge is issued on the day the learner earned it, not the day somebody pressed export.

### Interruptions on the record

`TrainingRecords.record()` now lifts the session's `interruptSummary()` onto the record itself as `interrupts: { answered, wrong, missed }`, instead of leaving it buried in `debrief.interrupts`. A proof rule should not have to dig through a debrief object, and should not break on a record written before the debrief existed — the mastery rule reads either, and treats an absent tally as "no interruption fired".

## The competencies

33 competencies (23 programme, 10 cross-programme) over 199 distinct stations and 68 standards.

Two tiers, one list. A **programme** competency mirrors a block in `WebXR/smartcity/js/curricula.js` one-for-one — same id, same stations — because a hall that buys the block wants one line on the transcript for it; `tools/check_competency.mjs` fails the build if the two ever drift apart. A **core** competency cuts across programmes: fall protection is fall protection whether it was earned on a tower, a scaffold or a bridge cable, and a worker should be able to prove it once.

`require` is how many of the named stations need a mastery run. Programme competencies ask for half their stations, capped at six — a twenty-six station survey block should not need twenty-six mastery runs to say something true. Core competencies name their number outright.

### Standards, and where they live

A standard lives once, in `tools/standards.json`, with `id`, `body`, `title`, `scope` and `source` (see `tools/briefs/proof-brief.md`). **That file is another team's work and is not in this tree yet.** So `competency.js` carries a fallback registry (`STANDARDS`) whose ids are the slug of body + title — the same id that registry mints for the same standard — with `standardSlug(body, title)` exported so the two can be joined. The moment `tools/standards.json` lands, `check_competency.mjs` starts cross-checking every cited id against it, with no follow-up commit needed.

Following the brief's rule, no clause number below is invented. `source` is `"verified"` only where the citation form is one we are sure of; a programme or practice named by body and title without a clause number is marked `"unverified"` and the UI shows a "citation unverified" tag beside it.

## The Proof tab

`Training records` → **Proof**. Three things, in this order:

1. **Competency cards** — one per competency the learner has any attempt on (thirty-three empty cards teach nothing). Each card carries a status chip (*in progress* / *demonstrated* / *consistent*), a meter and count of stations met against required, the standards the competency evidences, and the attempted stations with either "mastery on `<date>`" or the reason the best run there did not count.
2. **The proof transcript** — every attempt behind every claim: competency, status, when, station, score, stars, unsafe actions, interruptions answered of fired, time against par, whether it counted, and why not.
3. **The scoring rubric**, in a collapsible, read straight from `game.js`'s own constants so a learner can see why a run scored what it scored — and why a high score can still fail the rule.

Everything on the tab is built from plain data as React elements, never an HTML string: a crew tag, a station name and a standard's title all arrive as text and are rendered as text nodes.

When a run earns a competency that was not demonstrated before, two things happen: `Identity.emit("smartcitix:competency", …)` hands it to the embedding page (only ever to the learner's own home origin, per `shared/identity.js`), and the assertion goes through the existing LRS queue as an xAPI statement with verb `achieved` — so a competency earned on a kiosk with no network still reaches the Learning Record Store on the next connection.

## Export formats

| Button | File | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Export proof (CSV)** | `smartcitix-proof-transcript-<date>.csv` | RFC 4180, one row per evidence attempt, with the competency, its status, the standards, the attempt's score/stars/unsafe actions/interruption tally/time against par, whether it counted and the reason it did not. The spreadsheet a training director actually reads. |
| **Export competency badges (JSON)** | `smartcitix-competency-badges-<date>.json` | Open Badges 2.0 assertions, one per demonstrated competency. |
| **Print transcript** | — | A print-styled window built as DOM with text nodes only, never `innerHTML`. A transcript is the one artefact that gets printed, mailed and filed, so it is the last place to hand markup a chance to run. |

A competency badge carries, beyond what any assertion carries: the competency (`competency.id`, title, kind, status), every standard as an OB `alignment` entry (`targetName`, `targetCode`, `targetFramework`, `targetUrl`), the station ids it was demonstrated on, the attempt ids behind it, and the mastery rule text — both in `badge.criteria.narrative` and as `competency.masteryRule`. `check_competency.mjs` validates every assertion with the same `WebXR/verify/verify.js` a third party would use, so an exported badge can be checked in [the credential verifier](../WebXR/verify/index.html) like a station badge.

Like the station badges these are self-asserted by a static page: the ids are laid out as URLs under the learner's home, ready for a hall that hosts them, which is what makes hosted verification mean anything.

The xAPI statement for a competency uses verb `http://adlnet.gov/expapi/verbs/achieved` against an activity of type `…/activities/objective`, with a stable statement id (`smartcity-competency-<id>`) so an LRS de-duplicates a re-send rather than recording a second achievement. The whole Open Badges assertion rides along in an extension.

## The rubric

Straight from `WebXR/shared/game.js`; `check_competency.mjs` asserts the numbers here and in the UI are the engine's own constants rather than a second copy.

| What | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Score** | Each correct step in the procedure is worth 100 points times the current combo. A wrong control costs 25; touching a registered hazard costs 50 and is recorded as an unsafe action. Finishing under par adds 2 points per second saved. |
| **Combo** | Correct steps in a row multiply the next step: the multiplier is 1 + 0.1 per step in the streak, capped at 2.0x. Any wrong control resets the streak to zero. Combo raises the score; it never changes the star band. |
| **Interruptions** | An alarm that fires mid-procedure is worth 120 points, plus up to 60 more the faster it is answered. A wrong answer and no answer are treated the same way: the condition is still there, so both cost 50 and count as an unsafe action. |
| **Stars** | 3 stars: no corrections at all and finished inside par. 2 stars: at most one correction and finished inside 1.5x par. 1 star: anything else. Stars, not score, decide whether a run counts. |
| **Why a high score can still fail** | Score rewards speed and streaks. Mastery asks a different question — was it safe and complete — so a fast run with one unsafe action scores well and counts for nothing. |

## What this is not

A demonstrated competency evidences readiness against the standards it names, under the mastery rule. It is **not** a licence, and not a certification issued by OSHA, NFPA, ANSI, a state board or a union — those bodies certify people, and a simulator cannot. Every export says so in its own text.
