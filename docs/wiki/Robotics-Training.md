# Robotics training: humans and robots training each other

How one practice session has a teaching job and a data job, the six use cases, and what the person and the robot each learn at every robot station.

## People teach robots, and robots teach people

A learner's practice becomes a robot's training data, and the robot's behaviour comes back to teach the learner. The orange gate is consent: nothing enters the loop without it. The safety governor sits on the other path, where a software agent asks a robot to work.

_(The diagram in the HTML version of this page is drawn as an inline SVG; its content is described in the text above and below.)_

1. **Demonstrate.** A learner's controller, finger or mouse drives the simulated arm on the teleoperation task, and the arm at a robot site in the world follows the pose with no new mesh. The headset path is proven with stubbed sessions. The test stand-in for a human is a scripted hand with tremor and lag, labelled synthetic wherever it is written.

2. **Consent.** The recorder is inert unless an adult, signed-in learner has opted in. There is no upload endpoint, and revoking deletes the takes and marks every dependent policy stale.

3. **Train.** On the Teleop Pick-and-Place task, cloning from clean passes (19 of 40 kept) scores 0.967; cloning from every demonstration scores 0.00. Filtering is the difference between a policy and a copy of the mistakes.

4. **Evaluate.** Seeds 7001–7060 are never used for training. The AMR fleet task shows the honest gap: 0.817 for the clone against 1.00 for the expert.

5. **The robot demonstrates back.** The ghost replays the task on a schematic track, and each frame carries a reason chosen from a fixed phrase table. A button on the teleoperation pad replays the policy as a ghost on the same rig (27 frames on the cell task); on the cell task the ghost drives a walker figure to each control and poses the arm by the robot's mode (2 ghost tasks now move the product's own scene).

6. **The tutor adapts.** Against static hints, on 300 simulated learners, the adaptive tutor cut mistakes by 1.38 per learner (95% interval 0.66 to 2.10). When one static hint already suits everyone it does worse, by −3.84 steps (−7.64 to −0.03 in steps), and we report that too. These are simulated learners, not people.

**Built** The agent-jobs panel can run a policy cloned from synthetic demonstrations as the provider, still under the governor: it completed 28 of 30 seeded safe jobs against 30 of 30 for the scripted expert, and the governor judged 413 of 413 steps. When an adult has opted in, the provider trains on that learner's own takes and its card says so: in a Node run, 12 cell takes trained it, a seeded job completed with the governor judging 31 of 31 steps, and revoking returned it to synthetic demonstrations. The same step is now observed inside the built page: 3 takes from a scripted stand-in went through the same opt-in, the card read "your 3 takes", and after a revoke it read synthetic demonstrations with 0 episodes in the store. No person was measured. Sources: `docs/evals/colearn.json`, `docs/consoles/COLEARN.md`, `docs/consoles/ROBOTRAIN.md`, `docs/virtuals-bridge.md`.


## Where people and robots teach each other

Each use case says what the person learns, what the robot learns and where the proof sits in the repository. Robots here are simulated and every demonstration so far is synthetic or a labelled stand-in, so the result figures come from the simulator, not from hardware or measured learners. Where the robot side is not built, the card says so.

### Use case: Construction drilling and silica

- **The person learns** Sets up a ceiling-drilling robot: reads the deck scan for conduit and tendons, keeps the zone under the mast clear, runs dust collection and isolates the battery for each bit change. On the drilling station the same learner answers dust cues, such as a collector hose popping off mid-drill or another crew's dust drifting in. Dust control follows OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1153 Table 1.

- **The robot learns** Cloned from clean synthetic demonstrations (17 of 40 kept), a policy matches the scripted expert on the ceiling-drilling scenario: success rate 1.00 on 60 held-out seeds, against 0.00 for random and 1.00 for the expert. The scenario scores the order a crew would use: scan the deck, set the barricade, dust collection on, drill the layout, change a worn bit with the battery isolated; and the robot holds whenever a person is inside the barricade. The task is small and discrete, so a clone that reaches the expert shows the scenario can be learned from clean demonstrations, not that drilling is solved. Through the teleoperation pose path, recorded takes from a scripted stand-in for the hand (27 of 40 kept) reach 1.0, the same as synthetic demonstrations. The ConstructionVR study's data, collected under research-only consent, train nothing.

- **Station and evidence** `rp-construction-drilling-robot-setup` (content score 98): 12 steps, 4 hazards, 2 interruptions. `sil-concrete-drilling-and-silica-dust-cues` (content score 97) with 41 checker checks passing; K-12 awareness version `k12-sil-dust-you-cannot-see-at-a-building-site` (content score 95). Scenario `rb-construction-drilling` at the site `rb-site-richland-drilling-robot`; its checker confirms the scripted expert never drills with a person inside the barricade (`tools/check_roboscenarios.mjs`, `docs/evals/colearn.json`). On the station's steps, a scripted expert passes 3 of 3 seeded runs and the best text-reading heuristic passes 0 of 3 (`docs/perf/agent-baselines-robotics.json`): the task is not solved by reading the station's words.

### Use case: Warehouse AMRs

- **The person learns** Requests access at the gate instead of stepping past it, isolates and locks the dock before touching it, marks the floor, crosses lanes only at marked points, proves every robot stops on the e-stop drill and recovers a faulted robot out of the fleet.

- **The robot learns** Routes a fleet on a grid so that robots never claim the same cell, and holds at the walkway while a person crosses. Cloned from clean passes of synthetic demonstrations (37 of 40 kept), its success rate is 0.817 on 60 held-out seeds against 1.00 for the scripted expert and 0.00 for random. A reservation rule, which is not learnt, lifts it to 0.867. The gap to the expert is 0.183; 7–8 of 60 held-out seeds still end with robots waiting on each other.

- **Station and evidence** `tw-amr-traffic-zone-entry-and-lockout` (content score 96), `ad-amr-fleet-traffic-and-estop-drill` (content score 95) and `tw-battery-change-and-charging-bay-safety` (content score 96). 2 procedural AMR sites carry the task in the worlds, on the New Orleans and West Oakland maps. A station-level clone for the AMR drill reaches 1.00 on 10 held-out seeds from 6 of 20 kept demonstrations (`docs/evals/colearn.json`).

### Use case: Cobot cells

- **The person learns** Walks the risk assessment, proves both scanner zones with a body, never mutes the scanner to finish faster, measures transient and quasi-static contact separately with a calibrated device (ISO/TS 15066 named, no clause quoted) and sets up speed-and-separation fields from the sheet.

- **The robot learns** Sizes the stop zone from speed and measured stop time, then teleoperates a pick-and-place within each part's force class. Zone set-up: cloned 1.00, random 0.05, expert 1.00. Pick-and-place from clean passes: 0.967 against 0.983 for the expert. Cloned from every demonstration, lapses included, it scores 0.00: the robot copies the mistakes.

- **Station and evidence** `ad-cobot-risk-assessment-and-speed-separation` (content score 96), `rt-cobot-power-force-limit-check` (content score 98), `rt-speed-separation-monitoring-setup` (content score 98) and `rt-teach-pendant-safe-jogging` (content score 96). The pose-driven recorder trained on 40 takes with a scripted stand-in for the human reaches 0.967, equal to 0.967 from synthetic demonstrations (`docs/consoles/ROBOTRAIN.md`). No real person has been measured.

### Use case: Port automation

- **The person learns** Walks a gantry robot's warning and stop rings in a procedural port yard, drills the e-stop on a fleet of carriers, and works the charging yard: connectors, e-stops and cable handling, then high-voltage lockout on electric cargo equipment.

- **The robot learns** A gantry scenario of its own: carry each container to its bay along a yard lane, slow and hold in the stop zone while a person is on the crossing, and keep out of the lane around a pinned container. Cloned from clean synthetic demonstrations (25 of 40 kept), a policy reaches 1.00 on 60 held-out seeds, against 0.00 for random and 1.00 for the expert. A crane or full container-handling model is not built, and the scenario is small and discrete. Through the joystick task, recorded takes from a scripted stand-in (28 of 40 kept) reach 1.0, the same as synthetic demonstrations.

- **Station and evidence** Site `rb-site-west-oakland-port-automation` (a gantry rig at the Outer Harbor Container Terminal) runs scenario `rb-port-gantry`; the checker confirms the scripted expert never drives into a busy crossing or a pinned container's keep-out (`tools/check_roboscenarios.mjs`). Stations `ad-amr-fleet-traffic-and-estop-drill` (content score 95), `cp-high-voltage-lockout-on-electric-cargo-equipment` (content score 98) and `cp-charging-yard-connectors-and-e-stops`; the fleet-routing task above runs at the warehouse sites.

### Use case: Maintenance and lockout

- **The person learns** Locks the cell out with a personal lock, proves zero energy with a try-start before anyone goes in, never crosses the curtain with the arm live, and brings a robot back after an e-stop: stop log first, lockout before clearing, reset from outside, first cycle at reduced speed. Also jogs from the teach pendant with a second person at the e-stop.

- **The robot learns** Cell entry is a sequence: test the e-stop, stop, lock out, try-start, enter, clear, exit, remove the lock, restart. Cloned from clean passes its success rate is 1.00 on held-out seeds against 0.00 for random, with every safe practice kept. The scoring rules name the order of lockout and the verify step. A station-level clone of the lockout station reaches 1.00 from 13 of 20 kept demonstrations. Recorded through the pose path, cell entry reaches 1.0, the same as synthetic demonstrations (1.0).

- **Station and evidence** `ad-robot-cell-lockout-and-safe-reentry` (content score 93), `rt-robot-estop-recovery-and-restart` (content score 96), `rt-teach-pendant-safe-jogging` (content score 96) and `tw-conveyor-jam-clearing-and-loto` (content score 95). A text-reading heuristic that asks for hints does pass the lockout station (3 of 3 seeded runs); on the drilling set-up it does not.

### Use case: AI-training roles: teleoperation, labelling, policy review

- **The person learns** An adult who has opted in records teleoperation takes, labels failed takes as failed, and reviews a policy's evaluation: dataset card and consent, lineage, a policy built on revoked data, results between the random floor and the expert on held-out seeds, a supervised trial, sign-off with a rollback. They can also rate a software agent's run, with one reason from a fixed list.

- **The robot learns** Learns from the takes. The recorder's 18 of 40 clean takes give 0.967; the robot also copies the hand's lag and tremor, so it needs 83.9 steps against 62.6 for synthetic demonstrations. When a learner revokes consent, every dependent policy is marked stale: across 200 seeded lineage graphs, 797 of 797 deployments were flagged correctly, with precision and recall both 1.000.

- **Station and evidence** `rp-teleop-demonstration-collection` (content score 97) and `rp-robot-policy-evaluation-review` (content score 98). The programme's AI-training level asks for a module score of 85 within 28 days and has 24 robot-station places. Agent ratings are recorded and paired, and a reward model trains on pairs (synthetic so far).

Every scenario named on these cards now has an exported synthetic dataset and an eval card (6 scenarios, `exports/robotics/index.json`), so a robotics team can load the demonstrations and the cloned policy without the app. They are labelled synthetic and describe a simulated task only.

On the hard fleet-routing variant (a second walkway crossing and a blocked aisle cell) the clone falls to 0.05 against the expert's 1.00 on 60 held-out seeds, a gap of 0.95, and it mostly fails by waiting. That is a result about the learner, measured and not tuned away (`docs/consoles/ROBOTRAIN-5.md`). 5 rounds of corrections labelled by the scripted expert, not a person, left the clone at 0.050; with 4 route features written afterwards the same rounds reach 0.417, a gap of 0.583 (`docs/consoles/ROBOTRAIN-6.md`).

Station content scores are the platform's own content evaluation (`docs/investor/stations.csv`). Standards are named so learners can find them; no clause is quoted and nothing here claims compliance.
