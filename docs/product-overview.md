# SmartCiti.X — product overview

_Written 2026-09-23 from the repository as it stands. Every count is read from `WebXR/smartcity/catalog.json`, the output of `node tools/check_all.mjs` and `node tools/eval_content.mjs` run that day, `tools/standards.json`, `docs/ladders.md` and the feature guides under `docs/`; where a claim rests on a document's wording, the document is named. `docs/STATUS.md`, the generated series page, `WebXR/smartcity/README.md` and `WebXR/smartcity/REVIEW.md` still carry an earlier roster's counts; the catalog and the checkers are the current source._

## What it is

SmartCiti.X is a network of browser-based AR/VR training simulators for union trades and the work beside them. A learner opens a web page, walks into a generic but fully dressed job site, and works a real ordered procedure with their hands — a lockout, a confined-space entry, a chairside turnover, a pre-trip inspection, an isolation-ward setup — while one shared engine scores every touch, seeds real hazards, interrupts them mid-step with the kind of event that actually hurts people, and writes an auditable record. Every station names a real union and the standards the work is held to, from a registry in which a clause number is never invented. Above the game layer sit a competency tier with one mastery rule, ten-level ladders per programme, Open Badges and xAPI exports, an instructor console, a flow contract for a host platform, thirty-three device profiles and a robot embodiment layer. The whole thing is static files served over HTTPS; the one server it can use, an LTI 1.3 relay, is optional. Like the repository, this overview says what is verified and what is not.

## Features

**The procedure engine and the step kinds.** `WebXR/shared/game.js` is the one engine every app runs; rooms never score themselves. Eight step kinds — select, sequence, find, gauge, hold, track, turn, drag — cover what a hand does on a job. Scoring constants are exported once: 100 points per correct step times a combo that climbs to 2.0×, 25 off for a wrong control, 50 and an unsafe action for a hazard. Stars, not score, decide whether a run counts. The corpus holds 4,125 scored steps (median 13 per station), each with a `why` the brief requires to state "the consequence or mechanism a journeyman would give; never padding." A ninth kind, `drive`, is specified in `tools/briefs/assets-brief.md` and not built.

**Ten-level ladders.** Every programme is also a ten-level ladder (`docs/ladders.md`, generated by `tools/gen_ladders.mjs`, gated by `tools/check_ladders.mjs`): 290 levels, 256 at or over the fifty-step bar, 34 flagged partial and listed rather than hidden. Level N chains stations by a stated difficulty formula until its tasks total fifty steps; levels 7–8 carry an instructor-injectable interruption, 9 is a full shift, 10 a capstone of the hardest stations with no hints, no pre-brief and coaching ignored. A level passes only when one run of it has a mastery run on every task, and nothing else unlocks the next. A passed level earns an Open Badges 2.0 assertion and an xAPI statement, and claims no apprenticeship hours because no registry entry states an equivalence.

**Interruptions and hazards.** An interruption arrives unannounced mid-step on its own clock. Catching one is worth 120 points plus up to 60 for speed; missing one is an unsafe action. `tools/check_interrupts.mjs` proves 618 interruptions across 309 procedures fire, time out, score and visibly change the scene. The corpus seeds 1,282 hazards, each costing 50 and explaining its consequence. The console can switch a session between Assess and Coach; the choice is written on the record as `hazardMode`.

**The EI guide and the check-in.** `WebXR/shared/ei-guide.js` decides tone: name what happened in one plain sentence, say what to do next, never blame, never minimise. Every results card ends with an unscored check-in (Steady, A bit shaken, Need a minute); a rough run adds the station's `supportLine`. Answers stay in the learner's browser and are excluded from every export.

**Proof of training and competency badges.** Every finished run appends one immutable record — station, union, certification, score, stars, corrections, unsafe actions, time against par, interruptions, instructor actions, pass verdict — exportable as CSV, xAPI 1.0.3 and Open Badges 2.0. Above the game badges, `WebXR/shared/competency.js` holds **39 competencies** (29 programme, 10 core) over 304 stations and 69 standards under one mastery rule: two or more stars, zero unsafe actions, every interruption answered, time within 1.5× par. "No other rule earns a competency." The Proof tab shows competency cards, a transcript of every attempt behind every claim and the engine's own rubric; the verifier at `WebXR/verify/` returns self-asserted, hosted by the issuing hall, or altered.

**The standards registry and the eval.** `tools/standards.json` holds **320 entries across 88 bodies**, of which 261 carry a citation form the authors are sure of and 59 are carried as body and title, marked unverified. `tools/check_standards.mjs` gates: every station cites a registered standard in scope for its category. `tools/eval_content.mjs` ranks on eight weighted dimensions, printed worst-first, deliberately not a gate. On 2026-09-23 the corpus mean is **96 out of 100 over 316 procedures**; the five flat briefings score 73–88 by design and the lowest walkable station 90.

**The instructor console and flows.** `WebXR/instructor/` owns no simulation, scoring or records; it speaks the observer protocol (version 3) over a same-device `BroadcastChannel` or an optional WebSocket relay. Ten commands — roll call, note, hold, open, fire an interruption, weather, device profile, coach/assess, assign a programme or ladder level, send a flow — each honoured by the learner app, logged as an `instructorAction` and visible to the learner. A **flow** is an ordered graph a host hands over (station, programme, brief, check-in, gate and external nodes) with conditions on its edges; it chooses the order and never decides a verdict. `docs/flowhub.md` states that it is the SmartCiti.X side of a contract for Cognition.X's FlowHub "or any other" host, that no FlowHub specification exists in the repository, and that no host has been tested against it.

**Device profiles and controls.** `WebXR/shared/devices.js` describes 33 head-worn devices in five classes mapped to six run profiles; 22 are detected by user agent, 11 only by `?device=`, and a wrong guess changes only the rendering profile, never the procedure. Safety fields are vendor statements, marked unverified where unconfirmed. The controls layer holds one action table for five keyboard presets, a gamepad by W3C Standard mapping and 26 voice phrases, under two checked rules: hands do the work (nothing completes a step by speaking), and every action is reachable from the keyboard.

**Avatars and scenes.** Almost everything is built from primitives at runtime with procedural finish textures. Crew figures wear painted hi-vis dress; a checker fails one standing inside equipment. The one scanned model is the hub guide, CC-BY 4.0. 166 stations are indoor across ten interior styles; seven category districts add the horizon a trade would see. Two **scenic districts** landed on 2026-09-23, gated by `tools/check_districts.mjs`: `golden-gate-deck`, a suspension-bridge deck and tower in International Orange with a lane closure and the strait (53 of 120 budgeted meshes), and `bay-underwater`, a submerged pier and hull with a dive stage and a HUD chip for depth and bottom time (25 of 120). No station is placed in either yet.

**Robot embodiment.** `WebXR/shared/robot.js` is a software learner that plays every station headlessly for difficulty tuning. `WebXR/shared/robot-embodiment.js` gives every interactable a target pose, every step a grasp and force class, and every person a keep-out volume; a `noRobot` step is handed to the clinician with no credit. The fifteen dental hygiene stations are held to this by `tools/check_robot.mjs`; the careers and advanced stations are not yet in that roster. Datasets are labelled synthetic and engine-level.

**Records, xAPI, LRS, LTI and sign-in.** Records never leave the browser on their own; a configured LRS receives xAPI statements through a local retry queue. Identity is a launch context from a URL or an embedding page's `postMessage`, accepted only from the `learner_home` origin — provenance, not authentication. The LTI 1.3 relay (`tools/lti_relay.mjs`) verifies a platform's signed launch and is proven against a stand-in platform, not registered with a real one. The **sign-in layer** (`WebXR/shared/auth.js`, `docs/sign-in.md`) offers Google and Microsoft through client ids, an e-mail link through an endpoint the deployment operates, a device-only passkey and Sign-In with Ethereum; the page collects a credential and hands it to the host, and "nothing in this repository checks a password, validates a signature or decides that a person is who they say they are." Every value in `auth-config.json` is null by default, and `tools/check_home.mjs` proves that unconfigured it makes no request.

**Homepage.** `WebXR/index.html` is generated from the catalog by `tools/gen_home.mjs`: a card and deep link for every station, a section per category, a rail per programme, four app cards (SmartCiti.X, Trade Skills Simulator, Holodeck, Instructor Console), live search and sign-in. Hero counts are counted, not written; the checker proves every catalog string reaches the page as text.

## Deployment options

**Static only.** Serve `WebXR/` or the `dist/` folders over HTTPS. Records, progress, badges and check-ins live in the learner's browser; the instructor console works across tabs on one machine with no process at all. WebXR needs a secure context and, when embedded, `allow="xr-spatial-tracking"`. The CSP must allow `cdnjs.cloudflare.com` and Google Fonts, or both are self-hosted.

**With a host page or LMS.** The host supplies identity by URL or `postMessage`, receives progress, record and credential events at its own origin only, and may hand over a flow. The static catalog lets it index every station without loading the app. **With sign-in**, the host fills in the client ids or e-mail endpoint it operates and its server verifies the credential the page hands back. **With an LRS**, an https endpoint and credential are set from the records overlay, the launch URL or the embedding page.

**With a verified launch.** Deploy the LTI relay behind TLS and register it with the platform — a platform-side act not yet performed.

**With a networked class.** Run the observer relay on the room's network, behind `wss://` if it leaves the room, because the relay has no authentication.

**With hosted badges.** Host the BadgeClass and Assertion documents under the hall's issuer; the verifier then reports "the issuing hall stands behind it" instead of "self-asserted."

## What is verified and what is not

**Verified mechanically on every commit** (`node tools/check_all.mjs`, 33 checkers): all 995 shipped modules parse and 374 import only what they declare; all 307 simulators and 9 rooms build and play to a perfect run; every one of 618 interruptions fires, times out, scores and changes the scene; every station is reachable, inside its mesh budget and cites an in-scope registered standard; every competency and ladder level recomputes from the real modules and a level chain plays headlessly to a verified badge; every example flow validates; no voice command completes a step; the LTI relay refuses a bad signature, wrong audience, expired token, replayed nonce and unknown state; the console sets no markup; the homepage links every station and the sign-in module contacts nothing unconfigured; both scenic districts build, fit and are reachable.

**Asserted, and marked as such.** That the procedures are right: the review packet shows an automated preview pass on every section, a practitioner sign-off on none, and a roster stale at 235. The accessibility statement is a self-assessment for the flat mode with no third-party audit. Device safety fields are vendor statements. 59 registry entries are unverified by designation. The organisation attributions in the Job Readiness, Hunters Point and Civic Leadership editions rest on the text the repository holds and say "not sourced" where it holds none.

**Not yet done.** No headset performance pass has been recorded (the `?perf=1` instrument exists); hand-tracking is checked as a classifier, not on hardware. Badges are self-asserted until a hall hosts them. The LTI relay is unregistered. No host has been tested against the flow contract. The Holodeck's prompt interpreter and the Orbis-Stable client are seams with no live model. The bridge and underwater packs, the fleet and signage kits and the `drive` step kind exist as briefs. Nothing here names a customer, a partner agreement, a revenue figure or a quotation, because the repository holds none.
