Overview

A Superfund shoreline and the bay beside it, worked in the order a cleanup actually runs: the record first, then survey, then excavation and haul, then the groundwater that stays behind, then the water's edge given back — sediment out, tide back in, marsh planted. The first station is a sourced briefing on the real site and the data-integrity case at the centre of it; every station after it is a union trade doing its part of the work, and the discipline that made the case is taught as procedure: an instrument source-checked before and after, a sample that is the place it came from, and a split for a lab that does not work for you.

What a learner does

Each station is a procedure run in a 3D scene: pick the right control, put steps in order, turn a valve, hold a reading steady, drag an item into place, find what is out of place. Station 1 is different. It is a flat, sourced briefing and a knowledge check where the unsafe choices are wrong conclusions. Stations 2 to 26 are simulated scenes sited generically (a shoreline parcel, a remediation cell, a derelict switch room); none is presented as the real site.

Limits never appear as numbers. A station reads a monitor “against its action level”, mixes “to the label rate” or holds the cap to the design thickness “read off the permit”; the plan or permit sets the value.

How a run is graded

Stars. 3 stars: no corrections at all and finished inside par. 2 stars: at most one correction and finished inside 1.5x par. 1 star: anything else. Stars, not score, decide whether a run counts.

Unsafe actions. A wrong control costs 25; touching a registered hazard costs 50 and is recorded as an unsafe action. A wrong answer and no answer are treated the same way: the condition is still there, so both cost 50 and count as an unsafe action.

Programme completion: every station has a passing attempt (stars >= 2, no unsafe action).

Score rewards speed and streaks. Mastery asks a different question — was it safe and complete — so a fast run with one unsafe action scores well and counts for nothing.

The ladder and the competency

20 levels, 1,590 lessons and 122 tasks, with 20 of 20 levels full at a lesson bar of 75. A lesson is one station step under one condition.

Competency: Work a radiological and chemical cleanup inside the fence and at the water's edge, demonstrated by a mastery run on 6 of the 26 stations. A run demonstrates mastery when it earns two or more stars, records zero unsafe actions, answers every interruption it was given, and finishes within 1.5 times the station's par time.

Station 1 · Hunters Point Briefing

The record, as the briefing station sources it

Everything in this box is quoted or closely paraphrased from the dossier in hunters-point.js, with the sources that file cites. The station's own comment says the settlement and the lawsuit are live matters and its text is re-checked when the record changes.

What the site is

A former U.S. Navy shipyard on the southeast San Francisco waterfront, “on the EPA's National Priorities List (a Superfund site) since 1989”. The briefing says “The Navy is the lead agency for investigation and cleanup; the U.S. EPA and California's regulators (DTSC and the Regional Water Board) oversee and enforce the Navy's work”. It lists radionuclides, PCBs, heavy metals, petroleum fuels, pesticides and volatile organic compounds, “in soil and groundwater on land and in bay sediment offshore”.

Source cited: EPA Superfund site profile · SF.gov — HPNS cleanup

The data-integrity case

The briefing describes federal False Claims Act litigation that “alleged that employees and subcontractors substituted clean soil for potentially contaminated samples and fabricated radiological readings the Navy relied on”, at the Navy's radiological remediation contractor. It records a 2026 settlement of the government's claims in which “the company did not admit liability”, and notes that it does not resolve a separate suit by homeowners.

Source cited: U.S. Department of Justice press release (2026)

The neighbourhood's own air record

“Bayview Hunters Point is a residential neighborhood next to the shipyard fence.” The briefing points learners to the community air-monitoring effort as “the independent neighborhood air record” and states that “there is no on-site monitoring program of ours here”.

Source cited: Greenaction — Bayview Hunters Point

What the case teaches the crew

“independent split samples, chain of custody and third-party verification are the only reason anyone can trust a clearance number.” That is the discipline the other twenty-five stations teach as procedure.

Source cited: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.120 — HAZWOPER

The Guide

A help button on every page answers typed questions, and spoken ones where the browser supports speech, from a knowledge base generated from the repository. It holds a row for this programme, one for each of its 26 stations and 2 from its programme document, so a learner can ask what a station trains or where it sits.

Devices

The stations are WebXR pages. They run flat in any browser on a phone (touch) or a laptop (mouse and keyboard), and in immersive VR in a headset browser such as the Meta Quest line. The page detects the device, says how it will run, and can be pinned with ?device=. See devices.md.

Next steps for a pilot

  1. Would your team review every station, all 26, in order? Each one opens in a browser.
  2. Where a step, a hazard or the order of work does not match how your crews do it, would you mark it? Those corrections are what the prototype needs most.
  3. Is there a phase you would want to try first with a small cohort, on phones, laptops or a headset you already have?
  4. Are there stations missing from the sequence, or trades that should be named differently?
