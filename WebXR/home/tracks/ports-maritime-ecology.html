Ports, Maritime and Bay Ecology

ILWU, the Inlandboatmen's Union of the Pacific, IBEW port electricians, LIUNA and the environmental technicians who monitor the bay

OSHA 29 CFR 1918 marine terminal safety, the California Air Resources Board At-Berth Regulation, OSHA HAZWOPER 1910.120 and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers' Section 404 permit conditions for work at the water's edge

A working port and the bay it sits on, worked by the trades that share the same quay: cargo secured and moved, a ship cold-ironed instead of idling, fuel and lines handled without a drop or a hand in the wrong place, and a spill contained before it ever reaches open water.

What it proves

Competency: Work the waterfront without putting it in the bay

Demonstrated with a mastery run on 6 of its 11 stations, against OSHA 29 CFR 1918 Safety and health regulations for longshoring; CARB At-Berth Regulation for ocean-going vessels; OSHA 29 CFR 1910.120 Hazardous waste operations and emergency response (HAZWOPER); US Army Corps of Engineers Clean Water Act Section 404 permit programme.

These stations also count toward:

  • Confined Space entry, attendance and atmosphere (1 of this track's stations count toward it)
  • Crane and Rigging — the lift plan, the load and the landing (2 of this track's stations count toward it)

Badges

Each passed level earns an Open Badges 2.0 assertion naming the level, its tasks and their conditions, its lessons and the standards it evidences, with the attempts behind it as evidence, and sends an xAPI statement. Passing level 20, the capstone, climbs the whole track.

A run demonstrates mastery when it earns two or more stars, records zero unsafe actions, answers every interruption it was given, and finishes within 1.5 times the station's par time. One mastery run on enough of a competency's stations earns "demonstrated"; mastery runs on three different days earn "consistent". No other rule earns a competency.

Certification the work leads to: OSHA 29 CFR 1918 marine terminal safety, the California Air Resources Board At-Berth Regulation, OSHA HAZWOPER 1910.120 and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers' Section 404 permit conditions for work at the water's edge. No level claims apprenticeship hours: no registry entry states an equivalence.
