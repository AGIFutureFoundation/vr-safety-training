Culinary — The Working Kitchen

UNITE HERE Local 2 cooks, dishwashers and banquet staff; AFSCME and SEIU school and hospital food service

The FDA Food Code as adopted in the California Retail Food Code, the California Food Handler card and ServSafe manager certification, NFPA 96 for the hood and suppression system, Cal/OSHA's kitchen safety orders, and OSHA 1910.147 for every machine that gets cleaned

Fifteen stations in one kitchen, from the receiving dock to the dish pit. The hazards are the ones a cook actually meets — heat, blades, chemicals, cold, gas and the clock — and the standard behind every step is the one an inspector or a union steward would cite.

What it proves

Competency: Run a commercial kitchen to food-safety, machine-guarding and hood standards

Demonstrated with a mastery run on 6 of its 16 stations, against FDA Food Code, as adopted in the California Retail Food Code; NFPA 96 Standard for Ventilation Control and Fire Protection of Commercial Cooking Operations; OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 The control of hazardous energy (lockout/tagout).

These stations also count toward:

  • Lockout/Tagout and the control of hazardous energy (2 of this track's stations count toward it)
  • Confined Space entry, attendance and atmosphere (1 of this track's stations count toward it)
  • Hazard Communication — the label, the sheet and the dose (1 of this track's stations count toward it)
  • Emergency Response — the first five minutes (1 of this track's stations count toward it)

Badges

Each passed level earns an Open Badges 2.0 assertion naming the level, its tasks and their conditions, its lessons and the standards it evidences, with the attempts behind it as evidence, and sends an xAPI statement. Passing level 20, the capstone, climbs the whole track.

A run demonstrates mastery when it earns two or more stars, records zero unsafe actions, answers every interruption it was given, and finishes within 1.5 times the station's par time. One mastery run on enough of a competency's stations earns "demonstrated"; mastery runs on three different days earn "consistent". No other rule earns a competency.

Certification the work leads to: The FDA Food Code as adopted in the California Retail Food Code, the California Food Handler card and ServSafe manager certification, NFPA 96 for the hood and suppression system, Cal/OSHA's kitchen safety orders, and OSHA 1910.147 for every machine that gets cleaned. No level claims apprenticeship hours: no registry entry states an equivalence.
