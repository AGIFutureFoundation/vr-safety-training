Dental Hygiene — Unspoken Smiles

SEIU and UFCW dental and clinic staff, AFSCME public-health hygienists, and the ADHA as the profession's body

The CDC's Guidelines for Infection Control in Dental Health-Care Settings, OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1030 bloodborne pathogens and 1910.1200 hazard communication, the state dental board's practice act, the EPA amalgam rule (40 CFR 441), and the ADA's radiographic guidance

Fifteen stations that make a hygienist's clinical day — the operatory, the sterilisation centre, the chairside procedures, the emergencies and the outreach van — into scored procedures a training programme can run and record. Every step names the guideline it stands on.

What it proves

Competency: Deliver chairside hygiene under dental infection-control practice

Demonstrated with a mastery run on 6 of its 16 stations, against CDC Guidelines for Infection Control in Dental Health-Care Settings; OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1030 Bloodborne pathogens; OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1200 Hazard communication; EPA 40 CFR 441 Dental office point source category — amalgam separators.

These stations also count toward:

  • Hazard Communication — the label, the sheet and the dose (2 of this track's stations count toward it)
  • Emergency Response — the first five minutes (1 of this track's stations count toward it)
  • Trauma-informed Practice — consent, dignity and de-escalation (1 of this track's stations count toward it)

Badges

Each passed level earns an Open Badges 2.0 assertion naming the level, its tasks and their conditions, its lessons and the standards it evidences, with the attempts behind it as evidence, and sends an xAPI statement. Passing level 20, the capstone, climbs the whole track.

A run demonstrates mastery when it earns two or more stars, records zero unsafe actions, answers every interruption it was given, and finishes within 1.5 times the station's par time. One mastery run on enough of a competency's stations earns "demonstrated"; mastery runs on three different days earn "consistent". No other rule earns a competency.

Certification the work leads to: The CDC's Guidelines for Infection Control in Dental Health-Care Settings, OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1030 bloodborne pathogens and 1910.1200 hazard communication, the state dental board's practice act, the EPA amalgam rule (40 CFR 441), and the ADA's radiographic guidance. No level claims apprenticeship hours: no registry entry states an equivalence.
